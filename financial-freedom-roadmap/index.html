The four stages of financial health

Before we get into the roadmap, it helps to know where you’re heading. I think of financial health in four stages, each one a step further from relying only on effort income (we will get to what that is shortly).

1

Financial Dependency

You rely entirely on your effort income to pay the bills. If that income disappeared, you’d struggle to cover your outgoings beyond the next 6 to 12 months.

2

Financial Strength

You could last 1 to 3 years on the cash and investments you’ve built up. Leveraged or passive income may extend that, but either way it takes the pressure off if your effort income were to stop.

3

Financial Independence

Your investment engine covers the lifestyle you want, for good. Earning effort income is a choice. If it disappeared tomorrow, you’d be fine for the long term.

4

Financial Freedom

Your leveraged and passive income cover your lifestyle two, three, even four times over. This is where you’re truly financially free.

To move out of Dependency into Strength, Independence and Freedom, you need to build an investment engine. It’s easier than you might think. The Financial Freedom Roadmap breaks it down into three simple steps.

01

Maximise Income Streams

There are three ways to generate income.

Effort Income

This will typically be in the form of a job. You trade your hours for money, so your income is capped by the time you have.

Leveraged Income

This is income that isn’t tied one-to-one to your hours. Think of a side hustle, business or product that can keep paying you after the initial work is done. It usually takes more effort to get going, but it’s often something you enjoy more, and unlike a job it can scale beyond the hours in your day.

Passive Income

This requires little effort on a yearly basis. It’s a set-and-forget system that pays you over time, like investing in assets such as index funds or dividend stocks.

While it’s likely that when you are young most of your income will come from effort, the aim of the game over time is to move away from effort income into leveraged and passive income. You move into these income streams by investing in yourself (the biggest asset you will ever own) and investing in assets that pay you back over time.

But growing your income is only half the equation. You also need to free up money to invest it, and that brings us to step two.

02

Optimise Lifestyle Expenses

Many people spend everything they earn, and some spend even more. This is not the path to financial freedom. Optimising lifestyle expenses is not about never having a Starbucks or driving a dilapidated car your whole life. It’s about spending money on things you value and not mindlessly spending on things you don’t.

Do this well and a gap opens between what you earn and what you spend. That gap is the fuel for everything that follows, and it brings us to step three.

03

Build Investment Engine

Now you take the gap between your combined income streams and your lifestyle expenses, and you invest it. This is how you build your investment engine.

Your investment engine is your own mind and the assets you own. An asset is anything that generates a return to you. Every pound you invest goes to work buying more of these assets, and those assets pay you back over time.

Do this long enough and your leveraged and passive income grow to cover your lifestyle expenses. That is the point at which you no longer need to trade your hours for money. You can spend your life on work and leisure you enjoy, and that is what it means to be financially independent. Then if you keep fuelling your investment engine you become truly financially free.
